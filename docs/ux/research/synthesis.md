# Research Synthesis

## Similarities

- Multiple food-input methods are a category standard.
- Calories and macros remain the common nutritional core.
- Competitors reduce repeated logging through saved and recent content.

## Differences

- Logging ranges from diary-first to AI/photo-first experiences.
- Recipe functionality ranges from discovery to reusable logging utility.
- Products expose different levels of estimation transparency and correction.

## Learnings

- Faster input alone is not sufficient differentiation.
- Automation should accelerate input without removing user control.
- Recipe suitability should be visible rather than inferred.

## Opportunities

- Make calorie calculation a focused task rather than requiring a full diary workflow.
- Make calorie estimates transparent and easy to correct.
- Explain why a recipe matches the user's selected needs.

## Design Implications

1. Keep food and portion assumptions editable.
2. Present calories with relevant nutritional context.
3. Avoid unnecessary evaluative nutrition feedback.
4. Surface recipe suitability before recipe details.
5. Reuse common interaction patterns across both flows.
