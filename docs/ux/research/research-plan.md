# Research Overview

## Goal

Understand the key behavioral, informational, and UX factors influencing calorie tracking and nutrition-related decision-making, then evaluate how existing products address the two core scenarios: calorie calculation and suitable recipe discovery.

## Secondary Research Scope

Secondary research explored:

- calorie-tracking behavior and its potential effects;
- trust and reliability of calorie information;
- portion size and food-data accuracy;
- friction and complexity in food logging;
- how calorie information is framed and interpreted;
- factors that can support clearer nutrition-related decisions.

## Competitive Analysis Scope

Competitive analysis focused on:

- food and dish calorie calculation;
- food-input and logging mechanisms;
- portion and serving control;
- nutrition-result presentation;
- recipe discovery and search;
- filtering and recipe suitability;
- relevant interaction and visual patterns.

## Research Methods

- Secondary desk research
- Peer-reviewed and evidence-based sources
- Official competitor product documentation
- Feature comparison
- UX and interaction-pattern analysis

## Scope Decision

A separate market-sizing analysis was not included.

The assignment is focused on interaction design and two defined user scenarios, so research effort was prioritized toward user behavior, information quality, product patterns, and direct competitors rather than TAM/SAM/SOM, market revenue, or category-growth metrics.

## Limitations

This is a time-boxed desk-research exercise.

No primary interviews or usability testing were conducted. Research findings are therefore used to inform design hypotheses rather than treated as validated user behavior.
